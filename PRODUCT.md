# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Pups, furries, and fetish-community members in the underground scene. They access mosh.dog to share their links and discover others in the pack — socials, stores, events, fanpages. Context is typically mobile, after dark, in a queer or kink social space. Secondary users are community members browsing shared profiles submitted via Telegram or API.

## Product Purpose

mosh.dog is a self-hosted community link-in-bio platform for the pup and furry underground. It gives community members a shared space to publish and manage their links, with shared-profile submission and moderation for collaborative profile management. Success looks like a platform that feels native to its community — not a generic link tool plastered over with a dark theme.

## Brand Personality

Dark, leather, underground. Unapologetic, nocturnal, pack energy. The vibe is Recon.com or Mr. S Leather: confident heavy typography, night-scene palette, industrial edge — not playful-cute furry art, not corporate SaaS. The community is countercultural; the design should feel earned, not assembled from a UI kit.

## Anti-references

- Linktree: beige/cream/minimal SaaS aesthetic, cheerful pastels, hero-metric dashboard
- Generic link-in-bio tools: rounded-corner card grids, gradient rainbow accents, "connect with me" chirpiness
- Synthwave/cyberpunk: neon RGB, purple-pink gradients — too visual-noise, not leather-coded
- Material/Bootstrap admin defaults: the out-of-the-box Hope UI admin dashboard aesthetic already in the codebase should be replaced, not extended

## Design Principles

1. **Community-native, not community-themed.** The design should feel like it was built for this scene from day one, not a generic tool with a dark mode applied.
2. **Confident, not decorative.** Heavy typography and deliberate negative space over ornament. Mr. S Leather doesn't add gradient text; neither does mosh.dog.
3. **Night-scene palette.** Deep blacks and near-blacks, leather-warm neutrals, one strong accent. No warm-cream neutrals, no light mode defaults.
4. **Earned edge.** Motion, texture, and contrast used to carry weight — not to decorate. Every element should feel intentional.
5. **Pack-first accessibility.** WCAG 2.2 AA minimum. Dark-first doesn't mean hard-to-read; high contrast is on-brand here, not a concession.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 Level AA. Dark-first palette must hit 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. The community includes people who may have visual impairments or use assistive technology; accessibility is non-negotiable.
